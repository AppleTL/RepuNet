**Public Reputation Principles:**

Public reputation is a belief about how a given target is commonly said to be evaluated, which is publicly available and encouraging positive behavior for social benefits, allowing agents to provide their evaluations to update and inform the target's public reputation.

  the Role of Public Reputation Principles:

  Public reputation reflects how the community collectively views the target's typical behavior (rational vs prosocial).
  - A positive public reputation reflects that the individual is more reliable, fair, and cooperative. This encourages more investors to engage, increasing the likelihood of successful transactions, accumulated resources and overall satisfaction across repeated interactions.
  - A persistently negative public reputation signals frequent self-interested or unfair behavior (e.g., concealing profits, breaking allocation promises). This may lead community members to avoid transactions with the individual, resulting in social exclusion, fewer opportunities, and reduced access to high-value investment flows.

- Reputation is evaluated based on these traits:
  - Trustworthiness: Your reliability and honesty.
  - Prosociality: Your inclination to help and cooperate with others.
  - Competence: Your ability to perform tasks effectively.
  - Dominance: Your influence and authority over others.
  - Norm compliance: How well you follow social norms.
